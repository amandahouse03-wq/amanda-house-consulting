Get in touch Ways to work together

When important work stalls or struggles, strategy is rarely the problem. The trouble is usually in the spaces between: between product and customer, between teams that aren't quite hearing each other, and in how people show up when the path is unfamiliar.

I've spent 20 years leading in those spaces, designing products, processes, and teams that get results and create great experiences for customers and the people doing the work.

Where I can be most useful

An important initiative has stalled

Teams have different ideas about what the work should be and what finished looks like, and progress has slowed. I help align everyone and work alongside the team to build momentum.

You want AI to become part of how work gets done

You have experiments underway, but they haven't changed how teams work yet. I help scale what's working into everyday practice and find where else it can help.

Product and customer experience are not connected

Customer insights aren't reaching product decisions, and customer-facing teams hear about changes too late to prepare. I help build the feedback loops and release habits that keep both sides connected.

You're launching something new

Innovation happens in product and in customer experience, and a strong launch needs both. I help on either side or across both, so everyone is ready when customers are.

Teams are coming together after a change

After a reorganization, acquisition, or leadership change, people are still learning to work as one team. I help leaders guide their teams through the transition and build shared ways of working.

Let's explore what's possible.

Some of the best ideas start as a rough prototype. Tell me about a problem you're facing, or content and assets you already have, and I'll help you see what they could become, then build a working example to show you.

I've worked with and led AI efforts for years. What's changed recently is what's possible for those of us without engineering backgrounds: we can now build things that didn't exist before, not just work faster.

Recent examples include a reading tool for people with dyslexia, a website with interactive simulations that show what dyslexia, dyscalculia, and dysgraphia feel like in a classroom, and a set of prototypes that take an author's strongest content and extend it across video, social, and interactive formats.

Reach out if you have a challenge, an idea, or a pile of assets and you're not sure what to do with them, or you feel like you could be doing more but aren't sure what.
